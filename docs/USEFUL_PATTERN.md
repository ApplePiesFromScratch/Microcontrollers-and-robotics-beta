The break down.


Step 1: Reify a Relational Process
Take something that only exists through relations and treat it as if it exists independently. Make a verb into a noun. Turn a pattern into a substance.

Step 2: Use Relations to Define It
Now try to characterize this supposedly non-relational thing. You'll have no choice but to use relational language — comparisons, properties, locations, couplings, anything observable.

Step 3: Assert Non-Relationality
Claim that what you've defined is intrinsic, essential, or independent of all relations. Insist it's "really there" underneath the relations.

Step 4: Watch the Contradiction Emerge
The thing is supposedly non-relational (Step 3) but can only be characterized relationally (Step 2). You've stated its nature using the very relations you deny are constitutive of it.

Step 5: Defend with Meta-Moves
When caught, retreat to meta-positions: "Well, in this sense it's relational, but in that sense it's intrinsic." Deploy "in the same respect" clauses. Invoke mysteries. The paradox doesn't resolve — it just gets relabeled as profound.
